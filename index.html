Q-P2-001 Which forecast method performs best at each horizon?
▾
Direct Answer: At 1 Month, Driver GBM wins decisively (**WAPE 0.062** vs 0.105 Seasonal Naive). At 2 Months, Driver GBM maintains superiority (**WAPE 0.092**). At 3 Months, dynamic hybrid shrinkage (40% GBM / 60% Seasonal Naive) wins (**WAPE 0.089**).
Evidence: 9-origin rolling backtest evaluation across 288 monthly observations.
Recommendation: Implement horizon-dependent routing in FP&A planning tools.
Q-P2-002 When should FP&A prefer the seasonal-naive baseline?
▾
Direct Answer: FP&A should anchor to seasonal-naive methods for forecast horizons beyond 2 months when forward driver visibility degrades, and for contractual legacy divisions (BU_006, BU_008) where error reduction from ML is less than 5%.
Evidence: Multi-horizon error degradation curve where pure GBM degrades to 0.117 at Month 3 while Seasonal Naive stays at 0.105.
Q-P2-003 Are errors systematically biased upward or downward?
▾
Direct Answer: Seasonal Naive is severely biased downward (**-10.4% bias**), systematically underestimating revenue by ~$25M across 8 divisions due to missing organic growth trends. Driver GBM is virtually unbiased (**-1.5% bias**).
Evidence: Empirical forecast bias measured across 72 backtest test observations.
Q-P2-004 Which drivers explain useful predictive variation?
▾
Direct Answer: Revenue momentum (`rev_lag0`, $t=11.56$), annual seasonality (`same_month_last_year`, $t=11.07$), and price index adjustments explain 98.4% of total variation.
Evidence: OLS regression with HC3 robust standard errors ($N=288, R^2=0.984$).
